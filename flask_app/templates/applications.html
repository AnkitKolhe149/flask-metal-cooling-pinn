Heat Treatment Process Design

Engineers can simulate the complete cooling curve for any oil/steel/bath combination before committing to a physical trial. This enables rapid screening of new bio-based quenchants and bath temperature protocols to meet target hardness profiles.

  • Virtual quenching experiment design
  • Bath temperature optimisation
  • New oil candidate screening
  • Cycle time reduction
🔧

Residual Stress Prediction

Cooling rate directly determines residual stress state. Faster cooling → higher tensile surface stresses → reduced fatigue life. The cooling curve model feeds into residual stress calculations for gears, shafts, springs and other safety-critical components.

  • Fatigue life prediction
  • Distortion and cracking risk assessment
  • Component service life extension
  • Non-destructive stress estimation
⚙️

Digital Twin Integration

Embed the PINN as a real-time cooling sub-model within a Digital Twin of the heat treatment furnace. Live sensor data updates the model state; the twin predicts final microstructure and mechanical properties without waiting for lab measurements.

  • Real-time cooling curve monitoring
  • Alarm on deviation from target curve
  • Batch-to-batch consistency control
  • Predictive maintenance of quench tanks

🌿 Sustainability & Environmental Impact

The research directly supports the transition away from petroleum-derived quenching oils — a significant source of industrial hazardous waste. Key sustainability advantages of vegetable oils quantified by this model:

✅ Biodegradable & Renewable

Soybean and sunflower oils decompose naturally; minimal soil and water contamination risk vs mineral oils requiring hazardous waste disposal.

✅ Lower Residual Stress

The gentler cooling profile of vegetable oils produces smaller magnitude residual stresses, potentially extending component service life and reducing scrap rates.

✅ Higher Flash Point

Vegetable oils have a higher fire point than mineral oils, reducing fire and explosion risk in production environments.

📐 Learned Physics: The β Parameter

Unlike a black-box DNN, the PINN extracts the effective cooling coefficient β — a single number summarising the overall heat-transfer efficiency of the quenchant/steel system. This has direct practical value:

T(t) ≈ T_bath + (T₀ − T_bath) · e^(−β · τ)

Higher β → faster effective cooling → harder martensite Lower β → gentler cooling → lower residual stress

🚀 Future Directions

Extension 1

Multi-Phase PDE PINN

Replace the simple ODE with the full heat conduction PDE incorporating temperature-dependent thermal conductivity and phase transformation latent heat, enabling spatial temperature gradients to be modelled.

Extension 2

Residual Stress Coupling

Couple the cooling PINN with a thermo-mechanical model to directly predict residual stress state from quenching parameters — eliminating the need for expensive X-ray diffraction measurements.

Extension 3

Online Adaptive Learning

Deploy PINN in a production heat treatment line with in-situ thermocouple feedback. Continuously update β and model weights as new batches are processed, adapting to oil aging, bath contamination and seasonal temperature variations.
