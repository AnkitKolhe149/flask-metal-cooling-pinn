📋 Dataset Structure

Each row represents one time-temperature measurement from an experimental quenching curve. The data is structured as 24 cooling experiments — the full factorial of 3 oils × 2 steels × 4 bath temperatures.

ColumnTypeDescription
timefloatElapsed time in seconds
temperaturefloatSample centre temperature (°C)
oilcatSunflower / Soy / Mineral
materialcatAISI 1045 / AISI 4340
bathfloatOil bath temperature: 25/40/60/80 °C
tau (τ)floatDimensionless time = t / t_max ∈ [0,1]
theta (θ)floatDimensionless temp = (T−T_bath)/(T₀−T_bath)

Reading the curves: Each line represents a quenching experiment at a different bath temperature. Higher bath temperatures result in slower overall cooling due to the reduced temperature gradient between sample and bath. The initial steep drop corresponds to the nucleate boiling phase where vapour bubbles maximise heat transfer, followed by a gradual convection phase.

📐 Feature Distributions

Loading chart…

The raw temperature histogram shows a bimodal distribution — many readings cluster near the initial austenising temperature (~850°C) and near the bath temperature, reflecting the rapid exponential decay from high to low temperatures. θ and τ are dimensionless and show a more uniform spread suitable for neural network training.

🛢️ Cooling Behaviour by Oil Type & Steel Grade

Loading chart…

Plotting θ vs τ coloured by oil reveals that sunflower and soy oils have slightly higher θ at intermediate τ compared to mineral oil, indicating slower cooling — desirable for reducing residual stress. AISI 4340 shows markedly different θ-τ behaviour due to its alloying elements delaying martensitic transformation.

⚡ Cooling Rate Analysis

Loading chart…

The cooling rate profile captures the three quenching stages: (1) film boiling — slow initial rate as a vapour blanket insulates the surface; (2) nucleate boiling — peak rate as bubbles agitate the oil; (3) convective cooling — rate decreases as temperature approaches bath temperature. Mineral oil shows slightly higher peak cooling rates than vegetable oils.

The heatmap confirms that mean temperature increases monotonically with bath temperature across all oil types. Mean temperatures are very similar between sunflower and soy, while mineral oil shows slightly different thermal characteristics at higher bath temperatures.
